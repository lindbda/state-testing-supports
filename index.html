State Testing Supports: Quick Guide

For students with an Individualized Education Program (IEP) or a 504 plan (a plan for a student with a disability). Grades K to 5. School year 2026-27.

Start here

Due before Christmas break. All supports are in the IEP and reviewed.

Alternate Assessment box. If this is not done, please talk to your SEIF as soon as possible.

Check these 5 things for each student

  1. Class use: The student uses the support in class. An accommodation needs at least 30 days of use.
  2. State guide: The guide allows the support. The guide is the Usability, Accessibility, and Accommodations Guide (UAAG).
  3. IEP section: The IEP lists the support in the section named "Participation in Statewide and/or Districtwide Assessments."
  4. Testing form: The form matches the IEP. The form is the Nevada Department of Education (NDE) IEP/504 Testing Accommodations Form.
  5. Test day: The student gets every support that the IEP lists.

Your Special Education Instructional Facilitator (SEIF) fills in the testing form. Tell your SEIF if anything does not match.

3 mistakes to avoid

Reading passages (grades 3 to 5) Rare

  • Text-to-speech (the computer reads the test aloud) is not available.
  • Read aloud needs written approval from the NDE first.
  • Talk to your SEIF.

Support not in the IEP

  • Do not list it on the testing form.
  • The IEP and the form must match.
  • If a support is missing, the case manager (the teacher who manages the student's IEP) tells the SEIF. They ask for an IEP revision.

IEP lists both text-to-speech and speech-to-text

  • The online test allows only 1 built-in (embedded) support for each student.
  • Use text-to-speech in the test.
  • Use a second device for speech-to-text (the student talks and the words are typed).
  • The test administrator (the adult who gives the test) types the student's words into the test.

Rare This tag means very few students need this support. Talk to your SEIF before you list it.

  • If a support is not in the UAAG, do not write it in the IEP for state testing.

Make the IEP and the testing form match

These 4 items must list the same supports. A, B, and C are parts of the IEP. D is the testing form.

  • AIEP, present levels: shows what the student needs.
  • BIEP, supplementary aids and services: lists the supports used in class.
  • CIEP, participation section: lists each test support.
  • DTesting form: copy the supports from part C.

Check an IEP: steps

  1. Read the present levels of academic achievement and functional performance. Does the data show the need?
  2. Read the supplementary aids and services. Is the support listed?
  3. Read the participation section. Is each test support listed?
  4. Read the testing form. Check that it lists only the supports from the IEP.
  5. Open the Infinite Campus report:
    1. Click Main Menu.
    2. Click Student Information.
    3. Click Reports.
    4. Click Test Accommodations Default.
  6. Compare the IEP, the testing form, and the report.
    • If a support is missing from the IEP, the case manager tells the SEIF. They ask for an IEP revision.
  7. Give the checked forms to the test coordinator.

Remember: If a support is not in the participation section, it does not show on the report. Do not list it on the testing form.

Read aloud, text-to-speech, and speech-to-text

Text-to-speech for directions, questions, and answer options Type 2

  • Online. On paper, the same support is called read aloud in English.
  • Who needs it: Students who read slowly but understand what they read.
  • Rule: It does not read passages.

Reading passages in grades 3 to 5 Type 3 Rare

  • Who needs it: Students who cannot access the text. Example: a student who recently lost sight and does not read braille. Very few students.
  • Text-to-speech is not available.
  • Read aloud on paper needs written approval from the NDE first.
  • Talk to your SEIF about the NDE special request form.
  • The student takes the test alone in a separate setting.

Speech-to-text Type 3

  • Who needs it: Students who use it every day in class. Example: a student with hand movement problems, dyslexia, or a hurt hand or arm.
  • It must be in the IEP or 504 plan.
  • The student must know the software.
  • The student takes the test alone in a separate setting.
  • The student may need more time.

2 ways to use speech-to-text

  • Integrated: The device works inside a special test browser. Its name is DRC INSIGHT Permissive Mode.
  • Two devices: One device has the speech-to-text software. One device has the test. The test administrator types the student's words into the test.
  • Before the test: Mark speech-to-text on the student's record in the DRC INSIGHT Portal.
  • After the test: Delete all test content from the student's device.

The IEP lists both text-to-speech and speech-to-text

The online system allows only 1 embedded support for each student. Do this:

  1. Use text-to-speech in the test.
  2. Use a second device for speech-to-text.
  3. The test administrator types the student's words into the test.

Scribe Type 2

A scribe writes the student's answers.

  • Use a scribe for all items except the English language arts (ELA) full write.
  • The scribe must be a licensed Nevada educator.
  • The scribe must be trained for this year's test.
  • The scribe must sign a confidentiality agreement.
  • The student takes the test alone.

Scribe for the ELA full write Rare

  • The NDE must approve the scribe in writing.
  • The NDE rarely approves it. Use speech-to-text instead.
  • It is for students who cannot use both arms or hands, or who have major motor processing problems.

Special request form Rare

The NDE updates this form each year. It has only 2 choices: read aloud for ELA reading passages in grades 3 to 5, and a scribe for the ELA full write. Talk to your SEIF.

More supports

Closed captioning Type 3

  • It is for students with hearing loss.
  • It does not work alone. It goes with video sign language.
  • The IEP can say "closed captioning only."
  • The test coordinator must select both in the DRC INSIGHT Portal.

Large print Type 3

  • It is a paper test.
  • If the student can use the online magnifier, talk to your SEIF. The IEP team decides if large print stays in the IEP.
  • If large print stays in the IEP, the student must get it.

Support in the IEP but not given

  • This is not allowed.
  • Ask the student what helps before the test.

New for 2026-27

  • Patty paper is a new designated support for math. See UAAG page 12.
  • Sensory items are now a designated support. They were an accommodation.
    • Examples: fidget toys, stress balls, putty, foot rockers, seat cushions.
    • The team must say that one student needs it.
    • The item must not distract other students. If it can, use a separate setting.

English learners: the WIDA ACCESS test

WIDA ACCESS tests English skills. Use the WIDA Accessibility and Accommodations Manual as your main source.

  • Write WIDA accommodations in the IEP or 504 plan.
  • Do not mark WIDA accommodations on the testing form. Use the "Other" box on the form for WIDA exemptions and accommodations that are in the IEP.
  • No disability exempts an English learner from WIDA.

Domain exemption Rare

  • Use it only after the student has all the right supports.
  • The disability must still stop the student from taking one language domain (listening, speaking, reading, or writing).
  • A student can have at most 2 exemptions.
  • Write each one in the IEP or 504 plan.

Alternate tests

Alternate Assessment box. If this is not done, please talk to your SEIF as soon as possible.

Some students cannot take the regular test, even with supports. The CRT alternate test is the Nevada Alternate Assessment. The WIDA alternate test is Alternate ACCESS. Alternate ACCESS has a group for kindergarten to grade 2.

The 6 questions that must pass first

The IEP team must answer “YES” to all 6 questions before a student takes the Nevada Alternate Assessment. If any answer is NO, the student takes the regular test.

  1. Is the student eligible for and receiving services under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) through a current IEP?
  2. Does the student demonstrate cognitive functioning and adaptive behavior that limit full participation in the general education curriculum and state-wide assessments even with supplementary aids, accommodations, and modifications?
  3. Does the student require substantial supports to meaningfully access and achieve measurable gains on the State’s challenging grade-level content standards?
  4. Does the student require extensive, direct individualized instruction to achieve measurable gains on the state’s challenging grade-level content standards and to acquire, maintain, and generalize skills necessary for application in school, home, work, and community settings?
  5. The IEP committee’s decision about the student’s participation in the NAA was NOT primarily based on any of the following: a disability category or label, poor attendance or extended absences, native language, social, cultural, or economic differences, academic and other services received, educational environment or instructional setting, percent of time receiving special education services, English Learner (EL) status, current or previous low academic achievement, or current or previous need for accommodations (e.g., assistive technology/AAC) to participate in general State or districtwide assessments.
  6. Has the IEP committee informed the parent/guardian of the consequences of the student participating in the Nevada Alternate Assessment (e.g., modified diploma vs. standard diploma) and of being evaluated against alternate achievement standards?

These are the exact words from the state. NAA means Nevada Alternate Assessment. AAC means augmentative and alternative communication.

What the questions mean

  • Question 2: Low grades alone are not enough. The student file must show a test of adaptive behavior and a matching cognitive disability.
  • Question 4: The student needs this teaching at home, at school, at work, and in the community. If the team cannot say this, the answer is NO.
  • Question 5: A disability category does not decide it. Ask this: Is the student's problem with the regular test caused by the student's significant cognitive disability, and not by the other factors in the question? If yes, answer YES.
  • Question 6: Talk with the parent or guardian about the diploma type and the alternate achievement standards.

Source: Nevada Department of Education, Nevada Alternate Assessment: Student Participation Guidance.

Steps

  1. Answer the 6 questions. Use the decision trees to decide if the student is eligible. Ask your SEIF or test coordinator where to find them.
  2. Write in the IEP why the student cannot take the regular test. Write why the alternate test is right for the student.
  3. Do not decide by disability label, placement, or other category. See question 5.
  4. After the IEP is final, the SEIF selects the Alternate Assessment box in Infinite Campus, under Enrollments.
    • This decides which WIDA test list the student is on.
    • It is also for kindergarten to grade 2 students who take Alternate ACCESS.
    • The due date has passed. If this is not done, please talk to your SEIF as soon as possible.
  5. The SEIF checks the "Students Marked with Alternative Assessment" report.

Words to know

People and plans

IEP
Individualized Education Program. The written plan for a student in special education.
504 plan
A written plan for a student with a disability who needs supports in school.
Team
The IEP team or school team. The adults who plan supports for the student.
Case manager
The special education teacher who manages the student's IEP.
SEIF
Special Education Instructional Facilitator. This person fills in the testing form from the IEP.
Test coordinator
The school staff member who runs state testing.
Test administrator
The adult who gives the test to the student. The test coordinator is a different person.

Tests and forms

NDE
Nevada Department of Education.
UAAG
Usability, Accessibility, and Accommodations Guide. The state list of allowed test supports.
CRT
Criterion-Referenced Test. The state test in English language arts (ELA), math, and science. It starts in grade 3.
WIDA ACCESS
The state test of English skills for English learners. Grades K to 5. WIDA is the name of the test group.
DRC INSIGHT Portal
The online system from Data Recognition Corporation (DRC). The test coordinator uses it to turn on supports for each student.
Infinite Campus
The school's student data system.
Framework
A short summary of allowed supports.
Present levels
The part of the IEP that says how the student is doing now.
Supplementary aids and services
The part of the IEP that lists help the student gets in class.
Participation section
The part of the IEP that lists test supports. Its full name is "Participation in Statewide and/or Districtwide Assessments."

Support words

Embedded
Built into the online test.
Text-to-speech
The computer reads the test aloud to the student.
Speech-to-text
The student talks and the words are typed.
Separate setting
A different room. The student works alone or in a small group.
Noise buffers
Ear mufflers, white noise, or other tools that block outside sound.
Word completion
A tool on the student's device. It finishes words as the student types.
Flag for review
A test tool. The student marks a question to come back to it.
Video sign language
A video of a person who signs the test in American Sign Language.
Patty paper
Thin, clear paper for math shapes.
ELA full write
The long writing task on the ELA test.
Language domain
One English skill: listening, speaking, reading, or writing.
Domain exemption
Permission to skip one language domain on WIDA ACCESS. It is rare.
Administrative considerations
The WIDA name for designated supports.
Rare
Very few students need this support. Talk to your SEIF before you list it.

Sources: 2026-27 Usability, Accessibility, and Accommodations Guide (UAAG) and the CCSD State Accommodations and Supports training. Check the UAAG when a rule is not clear.
